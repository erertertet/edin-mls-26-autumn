\subsection{Checking GLM transcripts against alternative ASR models}
To assess GLM-ASR-Nano's transcription capability, we compare its output with three alternative ASR models on all 475 recordings (350 practice, 125 dev): 1,900 saved transcripts, including 32 reused pilot transcripts. Frozen text normalization aligns case, punctuation, acronym spacing and whitespace (e.g., ``I S A'' and ``ISA'' both become ``isa'') to diagnose agreement.

Levenshtein distance $d$ counts minimum unit-cost insertions, deletions and substitutions. Words use whitespace splitting; characters are Unicode code points, including spaces. For each query, NED divides $d$ by the longer sequence length in that unit, then averages queries: $N^{-1}\sum_q d(A_q,B_q)/\max(|A_q|,|B_q|)$. This length scaling is distinct from text formatting normalization, and is not a pooled edit/length ratio or reference-based WER/CER.

GLM differs on 71, 66 and 62 normalized transcripts, but averages only 0.31--0.35 word edits per query. Among the disagreeing transcripts alone, the means are 2.31, 2.36 and 2.35 edits. The low edit distances show substantial agreement across the saved model outputs.

Normalized top-5 sets differ from GLM's on 11.79\%, 10.95\% and 10.11\% of queries, respectively. On the separate 117-query dev evaluation, absolute cutoff uses 772--1,224 more document tokens than top-$k$ at 40\% descriptively matched recall across eight ASR/input settings. The retrieval token-efficiency trade-off persists across these models and input formats.
